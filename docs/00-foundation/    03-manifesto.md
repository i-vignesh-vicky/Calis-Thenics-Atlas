# Manifesto

We believe fitness should improve life, not consume it.

We build for people committed to long-term self-improvement, not short-term validation loops.

We reward consistency, discipline, learning, and contribution.

We reject product patterns that manipulate attention or encourage unsafe behavior.

We treat progress as personal and contextual.

We believe AI should amplify informed human decisions, not replace autonomy.

We design for trust, clarity, and longevity.

We build for decades, not demos.

> Help me stay consistent.

# What Users Are Really Buying

They're not buying:

* Workout plans.
* AI.
* Tracking.
* Analytics.

They're buying hope.

They're buying confidence.

They're buying proof that they're becoming the person they want to become.

The app simply delivers that through technology.

---

# What We Will Never Optimize For

We will never build features that encourage:

* Addiction to notifications.
* Endless scrolling.
* Vanity metrics.
* Artificial engagement.
* Fear of missing out.
* Toxic comparison.

Engagement is valuable only if it improves people's lives.

# Product Implications

Every feature we build should answer these questions:

1. Which user does this serve?
2. Which job does it solve?
3. Does it remove friction or add complexity?
4. Does it improve long-term behavior?
5. Would users still value this feature after using the app for five years?

If the answer to these questions is unclear, we should reconsider building the feature.

# Founder Principle

We are not building software for athletes.

We are building software for people who want to become better versions of themselves.

Athletic performance is one outcome.

Personal transformation is the real goal.

## Architect's Notes

This document reveals an important shift in our thinking.

Initially, we thought the product was for **calisthenics enthusiasts**. As our discussions evolved, it became clear that **calisthenics is our entry point, not our destination**.

The people we're building for share a mindset, not a sport.

They believe that improving their physical health is a way to improve their lives.

That realization is what makes the long-term vision—expanding to gym training, yoga, cardio, recovery, and AI coaching—feel coherent instead of scattered.
