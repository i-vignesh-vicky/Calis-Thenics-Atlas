# Document 03 — Core Users & Jobs To Be Done

> **Version:** 1.0
> **Status:** Founding Document
> **Purpose:** Define exactly who we are building for, what problems they are trying to solve, and why they choose our product. Every feature must ultimately serve one or more of these users.

---

# Introduction

People do not download fitness apps because they want another app.

They download them because they want to become a different version of themselves.

Our users are not buying workouts.

They are hiring our product to solve problems in their lives.

Understanding those problems is more important than understanding demographics.

This document defines the people we serve.

---

# Our Core Belief

We are not building for everyone who exercises.

We are building for people who genuinely want to improve themselves but struggle to do so consistently.

They are willing to put in effort.

What they lack is not motivation.

It is clarity, structure, accountability, and long-term support.

---

# Primary User Persona

## The Self-Improver

This is the user we optimize for first.

Everything else comes later.

### Who are they?

Someone who wants to become stronger, healthier, more capable, and more disciplined.

Fitness is not just a hobby.

It is part of becoming a better person.

They may train:

* Calisthenics
* Gym
* Running
* Yoga
* Hybrid training
* Functional fitness

Their chosen discipline is less important than their mindset.

---

## Their Goals

They want to:

* Build strength
* Improve health
* Master new skills
* Stay consistent
* Avoid injuries
* Look better
* Feel better
* Build confidence
* Develop discipline

Ultimately, they want a better life—not just a better body.

---

## Their Frustrations

They often experience:

* Information overload
* Inconsistent training
* Random YouTube workouts
* Plateauing
* Injuries
* Lack of accountability
* Losing motivation
* Not knowing if they're progressing
* Switching between multiple apps

---

## What Success Looks Like

After two years they should feel:

> "I'm stronger than I ever imagined."

> "I rarely skip workouts."

> "I understand my own body."

> "Fitness has become part of who I am."

---

# Secondary User Personas

## The Beginner

### Starting Point

* Never exercised consistently.
* Intimidated by gyms.
* Doesn't know where to begin.

### Needs

* Simplicity.
* Clear guidance.
* Small wins.
* Encouragement.
* Confidence.

### Product Responsibility

Never overwhelm them.

The first month should feel achievable.

---

## The Intermediate Athlete

This is likely where many early adopters—including you—fit.

They already:

* Train consistently.
* Follow programs.
* Know basic nutrition.
* Understand progressive overload.

### Their Problems

* Plateaus.
* Tracking progress.
* Better programming.
* Recovery management.
* Skill development.

They need insight, not instruction.

---

## The Advanced Athlete

Advanced users care about optimization.

They want:

* Better analytics.
* Fine-grained progression.
* Periodization.
* Recovery.
* Performance trends.
* Long-term history.

They don't want basic tutorials.

They want intelligent assistance.

---

## The Coach

Eventually, coaches become an important part of the ecosystem.

### Goals

* Manage athletes.
* Create programs.
* Track progress.
* Give feedback.
* Build reputation.

### Why They Matter

Coaches create value beyond their own usage.

One great coach can positively influence hundreds of athletes.

---

## The Mentor

This is one of our favorite personas.

Not necessarily a professional coach.

Simply someone who has trained consistently for years.

They enjoy helping beginners.

They answer questions.

Celebrate milestones.

Share knowledge.

Encourage others.

These people strengthen the community.

Our platform should recognize and reward them.

---

# Jobs To Be Done

People don't "use features."

They hire products to make progress.

---

## Job #1

> Help me know what I should do today.

The product should reduce uncertainty.

---

## Job #2

> Help me stay consistent.

Consistency beats intensity.

---

## Job #3

> Help me know if I'm improving.

Progress must be visible.

---

## Job #4

> Help me avoid injuries.

Longevity matters more than short-term gains.

---

## Job #5

> Help me understand my body.

Training is only one part.

Recovery, fatigue, mobility, nutrition, and habits matter too.

---

## Job #6

> Help me master difficult skills.

Examples:

* First push-up.
* Pull-up.
* Muscle-up.
* Handstand.
* Front lever.
* Planche.

The journey itself is motivating.

---

## Job #7

> Help me build habits.

Fitness should become automatic.

Not something that requires daily willpower.

---

## Job #8

> Help me connect with people who make me better.

Community should improve behavior.

Not distract from it.

---

## Job #9

> Help me leave behind a history I'm proud of.

People rarely regret keeping memories.

They regret losing them.

Years of workouts should become a meaningful personal archive.

---

# Emotional Jobs

These are just as important as functional needs.

Our users want to feel:

* Capable.
* Healthy.
* Disciplined.
* Respected.
* Supported.
* Proud.
* Improving.
* Part of something bigger.

---

# What Users Are Really Buying

They're not buying:

* Workout plans.
* AI.
* Tracking.
* Analytics.

They're buying hope.

They're buying confidence.

They're buying proof that they're becoming the person they want to become.

The app simply delivers that through technology.

---

# What We Will Never Optimize For

We will never build features that encourage:

* Addiction to notifications.
* Endless scrolling.
* Vanity metrics.
* Artificial engagement.
* Fear of missing out.
* Toxic comparison.

Engagement is valuable only if it improves people's lives.

# Product Implications

Every feature we build should answer these questions:

1. Which user does this serve?
2. Which job does it solve?
3. Does it remove friction or add complexity?
4. Does it improve long-term behavior?
5. Would users still value this feature after using the app for five years?

If the answer to these questions is unclear, we should reconsider building the feature.

---

# Founder Principle

We are not building software for athletes.

We are building software for people who want to become better versions of themselves.

Athletic performance is one outcome.

Personal transformation is the real goal.

## Architect's Notes

This document reveals an important shift in our thinking.

Initially, we thought the product was for **calisthenics enthusiasts**. As our discussions evolved, it became clear that **calisthenics is our entry point, not our destination**.

The people we're building for share a mindset, not a sport.

They believe that improving their physical health is a way to improve their lives.

That realization is what makes the long-term vision—expanding to gym training, yoga, cardio, recovery, and AI coaching—feel coherent instead of scattered.
